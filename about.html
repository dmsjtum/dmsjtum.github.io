About

§01Statement
Cartpole, Segway, quadrotor — the usual suspects, plus seven more.

I work on how robots decide what to do and how long to take doing it. My main line of research is trajectory optimization: given a system, a cost, and a goal, produce a motion that gets there well. The part I care about is the horizon — the duration of the motion — which almost every solver treats as a number you supply in advance and tune by hand.

That struck me as the wrong division of labor. If the horizon changes the answer, it should be part of the answer. HOP came out of proving that you can evaluate every candidate horizon at once, in closed form, rather than re-solving for each one. AL-HOP carries the same idea into problems with hard constraints.

A 40% recovery rate is not a triumph. It is a starting point, and I would rather report it than round it.

Separately, I build things that have to work on real hardware. My undergraduate thesis put a vision-language-action policy on a $200 arm and made it take spoken instructions, which taught me more about the gap between benchmarks and reality than any paper has. I try to report the numbers that did not go my way alongside the ones that did.

I finished a B.S.E. in Computer Science at Michigan and a B.E. in Mechanical Engineering at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. I am currently looking toward doctoral work in robotics.
