Research Experience
HOP: Horizon-Optimal Trajectory Planning
Jul 2025 – Feb 2026
Advisor: Prof. Zhongqiang Ren, Shanghai Jiao Tong University · RSS 2026 talk, undergraduate first author
• Proposed HOP, a new algorithm that makes LQR and DDP horizon-optimal: it optimizes the trajectory and its horizon

together instead of fixing the horizon in advance.

• Proved the Riccati recursion is a linear fractional transformation, so all candidate horizons share one computation,

O(N 2 ) → O(N ) even when time-varying; HOP-DDP handles nonlinear systems, up to 40× faster than brute force.
• Designed and ran the evaluation on four systems, from a double integrator to a 12-dimensional quadrotor.

AL-HOP: Constrained Horizon-Optimal Planning
Mar 2026 – Sep 2026
Advisor: Prof. Zhongqiang Ren, Shanghai Jiao Tong University · first-author manuscript in submission to ICRA 2027
• Proposed AL-HOP, extending HOP to hard state, control, and obstacle constraints: it alternates horizon selection on an

augmented-Lagrangian LQ model with trajectory optimization, perturbing the horizon to escape local minima.
• Returned valid trajectories on 100% of 500 runs (100 problems across 10 systems), vs. 79.8%, 79.8%, and 70% for
ALTRO and direct collocation with IPOPT and SNOPT; up to 4× faster on average at comparable cost.

PhysLA: Transferring Physical Supervision to Latent-Action VLA Pretraining
Researcher, Shanghai Jiao Tong University · second author, under review at ICLR 2027

May 2026 – Sep 2026

• Latent-action pretraining supervises contact and force only indirectly, and tactile or torque sensing covers only a small

slice of video. PhysLA learns physical targets where sensors exist, infers them from causal RGB elsewhere, and uses them
with visual prediction to supervise latent actions — no tactile or torque sensing at deployment.
• Designed two of its three stages and co-implemented them: learning horizon-structured physical targets from
pressure, tactile, and torque measurements, and transferring them to sensor-free video via causal RGB.
• Outperforms VLA-JEPA, the strongest latent-action baseline tested, on LIBERO and every SimplerEnv task group, and
beats both VLA-JEPA and π0.5 on each of five real-robot tasks.
